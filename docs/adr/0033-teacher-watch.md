# ADR 0033: Teacher watch: free self-consistency checks with a first-re-test rule, quarantine, and a training freeze

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-21

## Context

Every number Ratchet reports is agreement with the teacher: training targets, golden references, audits, certificates, savings. The code assumed the teacher is right, stable and deterministic. A hosted model at temperature 0 is not quite deterministic, and it can change without notice: another quantisation, checkpoint or routing behind the same model name on Token Factory. Night School would then train on shifted labels and grade candidates against them, and every gate would keep saying the student is fine, because it agrees with the new teacher on the new labels and disagrees with the old ones it has never seen again.

The cascade already pays for a check. Requests repeat, and a repeated request the student does not serve is labelled by the teacher again: an escalation or an audit. Before this decision `Ledger.add_example` bumped `seen` on such a duplicate and threw the second label away. The evidence tables of [ADR 0024](0024-evidence-foundations.md) reserved `teacher_checks`, `meta`, `examples.contested` and `examples.checked_epoch` for this item, and `ReleaseGate._tick_watchers` an empty last step.

The spec proposed comparing every re-test with the stored label and running `stats.e_bernoulli` over the comparisons. A review found that invalid: the comparisons that share one stored label are dependent. A request whose stored label is a minority answer (2.6% of draws when two draws disagree 5% of the time) makes every one of its re-tests disagree, so a teacher well within the tolerance looks broken whenever popular requests happen to have a minority label stored.

## Decision

`ratchet/teacher_watch.py`, with its ledger side in `Ledger` (one transaction per write) and its steps in the router, the release gate and Night School.

- **Checks: one per request per epoch.** A stored request's first re-test in a check epoch is compared with its stored label under the task's comparators (`Ledger._check_label`, from the duplicate branch of `add_example` in the same transaction, or from a Night School probe), recorded as a `teacher_checks` row, and `examples.checked_epoch` is set to the epoch. Later re-tests in that epoch are not compared. A disagreement marks the label `contested`.
- **What can be compared.** Both labels written by the same configured teacher; neither needing a redaction placeholder (two different raw requests can share one redacted text, so their labels may differ for a good reason); a registered task that names fields (`Task.verifiable`; whole free-text answers say little) and is not pinned (INV-10); both labels valid under the task's current spec (a stored label a tightened spec refuses says nothing about the teacher); and a label drawn at temperature 0 without a client's reasoning effort (`router.comparable_label`). A sampled label is stored with `checked_epoch = -1` (`ledger.UNCHECKABLE`) and never compared, nor is a sampled re-test.
- **Why the rule makes the e-process valid.** H0: for every request, two independent teacher labels disagree with probability at most ε (`teacher_noise_max`, 5%). Each check compares two independent draws, the stored label and the first re-test, and the checks of an epoch are of distinct requests, so given every earlier check each new one disagrees with probability at most ε. That is exactly the condition under which the mixture e-value `stats.e_bernoulli(D, N, ε, 'above')` is a non-negative supermartingale (its docstring proves it for any conditional rate at or below ε), and Ville's inequality bounds the chance that it ever reaches 1/α by α. By seeded Monte Carlo (100 requests, two draws disagreeing exactly 5% of the time, 300 runs): comparing the stored label with ten re-tests alarmed in 30% of runs; the first re-test alone in none.
- **Labels selected by a disagreement are not compared again.** A contested label was picked out by a disagreement: given one, either label of a 50/50 pair is as likely, so comparing it again in a later epoch counts about one disagreement in two under H0. Contested labels are therefore skipped, and an adjudicated label (a majority chosen after a disagreement, not one draw) is marked UNCHECKABLE. A label that agreed before may be compared again in a later epoch: conditioning on an agreement only lowers the chance of the next disagreement (sum p³ / sum p² ≥ sum p², by Cauchy–Schwarz).
- **Selection.** The argument also needs that whether a request is re-tested does not depend on which label is stored. Probes go in hash order, audits are random draws, and cold-start escalations depend on the request only. Escalations by confidence depend on the student, which was trained on stored labels, so a minority label could change the student's confidence on that very request; this is second order and stated as an assumption.
- **The freeze rule (FR-6.42).** The e-process is restarted every ledger day of the epoch and the restarts are mixed: the e-process started at the j-th day's first check has weight 1/(j(j+1)) (the weights sum to 1; one not started counts 1). The start times are calendar days, fixed in advance, so the mixture is still a supermartingale that starts at 1 and the bound still holds, at the price of a factor 2 on the first day's e-process. A single e-process over a long epoch would first have to win back what weeks of agreeing checks cost it: by Monte Carlo, after 400 agreeing checks over 14 days and then 15% disagreement, the single e-process did not alarm within 200 checks, the mixture after 129. With checks at 5% and 3% disagreement over 10 days, the mixture alarmed in 0.7% and 0% of 300 runs (α = 1%); at 20% it alarmed after a median of 52 checks. When the e-value reaches 1/`alpha_teacher` (100), `ReleaseGate._tick_watchers` sets `meta` `teacher_frozen` and writes one `teacher_drift` event (`Ledger.teacher_freeze`, one transaction that re-checks the epoch, so the gateway and the nightly job write it once). The event names the stretch of days that weighs most, its counts and the e-value. The tick reads two indexed lookups when nothing changed: the epoch in `meta` and the check rows after the last id it read.
- **The freeze and the ack.** A frozen Night School writes a `skip` event ("teacher labels look unstable") before any teacher call or training, before the in-flight check, because only an operator can lift it: `POST /ratchet/teacher/ack` (admin key in live mode, open in mock mode without admin keys, like the other management calls) starts a new epoch, unfreezes and writes `teacher_ack` with the admin's name. The guarantee is per epoch; an operator who acks restarts it knowingly. Serving is never affected.
- **Model ids.** `Ratchet._ask_teacher` compares the `model` of each primary-teacher response with the last one (a string comparison in memory, loaded from `meta` at startup); a change goes to `Ledger.teacher_model_seen`, which writes `teacher_model_changed` and starts a new epoch, so every stored label can be re-tested once against the new model. An id already reported in the current epoch only becomes the last one: a teacher alternating between two backends with different ids starts one epoch and writes one event, not one per response. The fallback teacher's ids are not watched (they differ by design). A ledger error there never fails the teacher's answer. The id is the upstream's string and the event reaches the public `/ratchet/stats`, so `teacher_model_seen` keeps it only when it is a model id (`upstream.model_id`: the pattern in full, no `://`) and otherwise records the fixed `unrecognized-model-id`.
- **Quarantine and adjudication (FR-5.41).** `dataset.rows` leaves contested rows out of both splits (`excluded_contested` in the manifest). Each night, unless frozen, up to `RATCHET_ADJUDICATE_MAX` (50) contested labels get two more answers from the same teacher; a strict majority of the three canonical answers becomes the label and ends the quarantine. The same teacher wrote it, so provenance and license do not change (INV-1, INV-2), and the new label passed the same checks as any stored answer (complete, valid under the spec, no placeholder needed; INV-12, INV-16). Without a majority (1-1-1) the label stays quarantined as UNRESOLVED (`contested = 2`) and later nights adjudicate it after the labels never adjudicated, so requests the teacher is split on cannot take every night's budget.
- **Probes (FR-5.40).** Traffic with few repeats gives few checks, and a student that serves most requests leaves few re-tests to the teacher. Each night, unless frozen, Night School asks the teacher again about up to `RATCHET_TEACHER_PROBES` (20) stored labels that can be checked and were not re-tested in this epoch, in hash order, the way an audit asks (temperature 0, the example's stored conditions). An answer that could not be stored (cut off, invalid, needing a placeholder) is no check. Probes and adjudication run while a release is in flight too: they are evidence, not training (INV-5). The first teacher error stops both, not the night. The freeze rule runs again right after them.
- **The ceiling (FR-6.43).** Per request with label probabilities p_y, a teacher agrees with itself with probability a = sum p_y², and any student, which can at best always give the most likely label, agrees with a fresh teacher label with probability at most max p_y ≤ sqrt(a). Over requests, E[max p] ≤ E[sqrt(a)] ≤ sqrt(E[a]) = sqrt(A) by Jensen. The golden gate writes an advisory `warning` when an epoch has at least 30 checks and `min_precision` is above sqrt of the upper 95% Wilson bound of A: no student can meet the floor at full coverage against this teacher. It explains a run of rejections; it decides nothing. A student that abstains can do better on what it serves, so the bound is about full coverage only.
- **The BOM.** Each BOM written by `run_night` has a `teacher` section (`bom.EXTRA_KEYS`): the epoch, its checks and disagreements, the self-agreement and how many contested labels that night's sets left out.
- **The switch.** `RATCHET_TEACHER_WATCH=0` turns off everything that acts (quarantine, freeze, warning, probes, adjudication). The checks are still recorded, at one indexed lookup per stored duplicate, so turning it on later has history.
- **Defaults keep every number.** The mock teacher only changes labels when `noise` > 0 (`MockTeacher(noise=...)`, `POST /ratchet/demo/teacher-drift`, `python -m sim.simulate --teacher-noise`), each draw from `_rng('teacher-noise', text, calls)`, never a shared generator. At noise 0 nothing is drawn, every re-test and probe agrees, nothing is contested and no teacher event is written, so the reference simulation and the three pinned workloads are byte-identical, and a run with the watch off has the same days, versions and events.

## Consequences

- Teacher drift becomes a measured, bounded signal: a stable teacher freezes training with probability at most 1% per epoch; an unstable one is caught from free re-tests plus about 20 probe calls a night.
- Night School can stop training on a day it would have trained, and needs an operator to resume. The runbook says what to check first (the model id) and when to raise `RATCHET_TEACHER_NOISE_MAX` on purpose.
- A few labels leave training and the golden set while contested. Adjudication brings most back within a night or two.
- An adjudicated label changes the example's `completion`. Committed training sets (FR-16.4, ADR 0027) prove membership from the leaves written at training time (`version_leaves`, append-only), so an older adapter's proofs do not move; a later adapter commits to the new label.
- Nightly watch calls are paid teacher calls (at most `RATCHET_TEACHER_PROBES` + 2 × `RATCHET_ADJUDICATE_MAX`, about $0.13 at the defaults and list prices, unverified). The night's `teacher_watch` event states the spend, but the cost report does not count it yet: the `spend` table (ADR 0024) belongs to the clean-room backfill item, which adds its writer; this spend should go there too.
- A teacher check is pooled across tasks and across configured teachers that store labels (a fallback teacher with `RATCHET_STORE_FALLBACK_TEACHER=1` is checked against its own labels). Per-task e-values in `GET /ratchet/teacher` are diagnostic only.
- The request path gains one indexed lookup per stored duplicate (inside the write `add_example` already makes) and a string comparison per teacher call.

## Alternatives

- **Compare every re-test with the stored label** (the spec's first design): invalid, as above; 30% false alarms in the Monte Carlo at the tolerated rate.
- **Compare consecutive re-tests with each other instead of the stored label.** Each pair would be two fresh draws, but consecutive pairs share a draw, so they are dependent the same way; disjoint pairs would halve the checks. The first-re-test rule uses the stored label, which is the label that matters for training.
- **A single e-process per epoch** (the spec): valid, but it has to win back the cost of every agreeing check before an alarm, so a teacher that changes late in a long epoch is caught late or never. The day mixture costs a constant factor and keeps the bound.
- **Freeze on a fixed disagreement rate** ("more than 5% of the last 100 checks"): easy to read, but repeated looks inflate its error rate without bound; the e-process can be checked on every tick.
- **Adjudicate with another model** (the judge): its labels would change the provenance of training targets and need their own license check (INV-1). The same teacher keeps the provenance; a 2-of-3 majority of one teacher removes its noise, not its bias, and the freeze handles bias.
- **Store the second label as a second example.** It would double-count a request in training and break `seen`; the comparison is evidence, not data.
