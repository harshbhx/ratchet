## The problem

- **LLM bills grow with traffic.** Enterprise spend on model APIs was $12.5B in 2025 ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/), 2025 report). Much of what teams send is narrow and repetitive: classification, extraction, ticket triage, steps inside agents.
- **The obvious fix is often not allowed.** Training a cheaper model on a closed model's answers breaks the terms of the largest vendors: [OpenAI Business Terms §3.3(e)](https://openai.com/policies/business-terms/) and [Anthropic Commercial Terms D.4(a)](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms) both forbid using outputs to train competing models. A tool that distills that traffic puts its users at risk.
- **Doing it by hand is slow.** Logging, cleaning, fine-tuning, evaluating, deploying and watching for regressions are separate chores, and most tools cover one of them.

## Who it is for

Teams with steady, repetitive LLM traffic on tasks a small model can learn, for example support triage, field extraction, routing and classification, or the many small steps inside an agent. It is a poor fit for open-ended work where every request is different, and for very low volume (see [Cost and savings](/guides/cost-and-savings/) for the break-even).

## The value

- **Cost falls as the student takes over.** The student answers when it is confident and the open teacher answers the rest. Every request records what it cost and what the teacher alone would have cost.
- **You own the result.** The product is a LoRA adapter on an open base model, plus a signed bill of materials that lists the teacher and license of every training example.
- **License-clean by design.** Closed teachers are refused in code, and each example's teacher and license are recorded when it is captured. See [Licenses](/guides/licenses/).
- **Releases are gated.** A new student must beat the current one on held-out data, then pass shadow and canary stages, and rolls back automatically if it slips. See [The release gate](/guides/release-gate/).
- **Savings are measured, not promised.** In the reference simulation (mock models, ticket triage), the all-teacher cost was about $1.09 per 1,000 requests and Ratchet's steady-state cost about $0.15 to $0.16, a saving of 85 to 87% (simulation, see [Cost and savings](/guides/cost-and-savings/)). Real savings depend on the traffic mix and on GPU utilization, and Ratchet's cost report says when a GPU endpoint would cost more than it saves.

## How it could make money

The plan is an open-source core (Apache-2.0: gateway, ledger, Night School, release gate, dashboard) that anyone can self-host, plus a managed or enterprise offering for teams that do not want to run the nightly loop and GPUs themselves. The idea for the managed tier is to charge a share of savings that Ratchet can verify, with no fee when there are no savings; the code already produces signed daily savings receipts for this (see [Evidence](/guides/evidence/)). This is a plan, not a product: nothing is sold today.

## Why now

- **A deadline for fine-tuning customers.** OpenAI lists no new self-serve fine-tuning jobs after Jan 6, 2027 ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations), checked Oct 21, 2026). Teams with fine-tunes there need somewhere else to run them.
- **Small models are being used for narrow tasks.** NVIDIA Research estimates that 40 to 70% of LLM calls in agent systems could go to small models ([arXiv 2506.02153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02153), an estimate, not a measurement).

## How it differs

| Approach | What it does well | What it does not do | Ratchet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routers and gateways | Pick a model per request, log and govern traffic | Learn from the traffic; every call stays rented | Learns from the traffic and trains a student |
| Fine-tuning and distillation services | Train a model when you start a job | Run by themselves every night, or record each example's teacher license | Retrains nightly and signs the lineage into the adapter |
| Reference blueprints and tutorials | Show the pattern and its parts | Run unattended; promotion is left to a person | Gates, canaries and rolls back automatically |

Each of these exists in good form; the pieces are prior art. What Ratchet adds is the combination in one loop that runs by itself, with license-checked teachers and a statistical release gate. Sources are listed in [What is Ratchet](/guides/what-is-ratchet/).

## Honest limits

<Aside type="caution" title="As of Oct 21, 2026">
- **Simulation only.** Ratchet has not run on a real Nebius account. All savings figures above come from mock models and are labelled as simulation. Live numbers will replace them after a published live run.
- **One developer.** There is no team, no customer and no revenue.
- **Savings depend on the traffic mix.** Per-token savings against the cheapest tiers of closed models are small; the large savings come from moving traffic off top-tier models, and only for tasks a small model can learn.
- **A Token Factory terms question is open.** A question about its Terms of Service §5(f) was sent to Nebius support on Oct 19, 2026. This is not legal advice.
</Aside>
